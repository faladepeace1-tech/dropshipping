Is a Done-For-You Dropshipping Store Worth It?

By Akinyemi Ifeoluwa · August 6, 2026 · 7 min read

A done-for-you store can save months of trial and error, or it can be an expensive lesson. The difference is usually not the price. It is whether the service actually hands over a working asset.

What you are really buying

You are not buying a guarantee of sales. You are buying time, technical execution and decisions: niche research, product selection, store design, supplier setup, payment integration and a handover process. If the provider cannot explain those deliverables clearly, the offer is vague.

When it is worth it

  • You have a budget for the build and a separate budget for traffic.
  • You want to launch faster than you could learn everything yourself.
  • You value a handover walkthrough and a clean technical setup.
  • You are prepared to handle customers, content and marketing after launch.
  • You want to own the accounts and data, not rent them.

When it is not worth it

  • You expect guaranteed profit or passive income.
  • You have no budget left for ads or content after the build.
  • You are not willing to answer customers or make decisions.
  • You have not checked what is included and what is extra.
  • You are buying only because the sales page creates panic.

How to compare providers

Ask the same five questions to every provider:

  1. What exactly is included, in writing?
  2. Who owns the domain, store account and customer data?
  3. What happens if a supplier or payment gateway rejects the store?
  4. How many revision rounds are included?
  5. What support do I get after handover, and for how long?

A provider who answers clearly is safer than one who only shows lifestyle screenshots. Ask for a walkthrough of a real store, not just a theme demo.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed income claims.
  • Pressure to pay outside the checkout or in crypto only.
  • No refund or revision policy.
  • No clear business identity or contact channel.
  • Proof that cannot be verified.

The hidden work after handover

A store is not a business by itself. After launch you still need traffic, customer service, content and product decisions. A good provider should explain this honestly instead of implying the hard part is over.

Frequently asked questions

Is done-for-you better than a course?

A course teaches you to do the work. Done-for-you does the technical work for you. Choose based on time, budget and how much you want to learn.

Can I still make changes after handover?

Yes. The store is yours. You can edit products, pages and settings. Support covers the agreed build scope.

Do I need to be in Nigeria?

No. Nexatech is based in Lagos and serves clients worldwide. The target market matters more than your location.

What done-for-you should include

A complete done-for-you engagement should cover product research, store setup, supplier connections, payment configuration, tracking, policies, basic training and a handover process. It should also define what happens after launch: revision window, support channel and response time.

Ask for the deliverables in writing. "We build your store" is not a scope. A list of pages, integrations, products and training sessions is.

DIY versus done-for-you: a fair comparison

DIY is cheaper in cash but costs time, mistakes and learning curve. Done-for-you is cheaper in time but requires trust and a clear scope. Compare the total cost of both paths, including the revenue you might lose while learning and the cost of fixing a poorly built store later.

How to evaluate an agency

  • Ask for live store examples and a walkthrough.
  • Ask who will do the work and who owns the accounts.
  • Check the contract for scope, timeline, revisions and refunds.
  • Ask how they handle supplier failures and payment issues.
  • Ask what training and support you receive after handover.

What you should still own

You should own the domain, store account, ad account, pixel, payment account and content. You should be able to remove the agency without losing the business. If an agency insists on holding accounts, ask why and get the exit process in writing.

A handover checklist

Confirm admin access, product data, supplier contacts, payment settings, tracking, policies, passwords and documentation. Record a short training session and store it somewhere you can find later. A proper handover is part of the product, not an afterthought.

When to hire help after launch

Consider help when you have consistent orders but no time to improve the store, when support volume is overwhelming or when ad performance has plateaued. Hire for a specific bottleneck, not because you feel you should be doing more.

What a good discovery call looks like

A serious provider asks about your budget, market, experience, product interests and goals before quoting. They explain what they can and cannot do, and they are honest about the work required from you. A call that is only a sales pitch is a warning sign.

How to set success criteria

Define what a successful handover means: store live, products loaded, payments tested, tracking working, policies published and training completed. These are delivery criteria, not revenue guarantees. A provider should be willing to be judged against them.

The true cost of a cheap build

A cheap build often becomes expensive when you pay again to fix tracking, rewrite product pages, reconnect payments or rebuild a broken theme. Compare the total cost of ownership, not the first invoice. Ask what is excluded and how much those exclusions would cost to solve.

A decision worksheet

List your budget, available time, technical comfort, target launch date and the tasks you genuinely enjoy. If the list shows you cannot handle setup and operations, done-for-you may save you months. If you have time and enjoy the work, DIY can be a reasonable learning path.

What to prepare before hiring

Write down your budget, target market, product interests, preferred payment methods and launch deadline. Gather any brand assets, domain details and supplier contacts you already have. A prepared brief reduces discovery time and helps you compare providers fairly.

How to manage the project

Agree on milestones, a communication channel and a weekly update. Review work at each milestone instead of waiting until the end. Raise scope changes early and confirm any change to cost or timeline in writing.

Post-handover support

Ask what support covers and for how long. A short support window is normal, but you should know how to get help with a broken checkout, a payment issue or a supplier problem. Save the documentation and record a walkthrough you can revisit.

What a good scope document contains

A clear scope names the products, pages, integrations, copy, images, tracking and training included. It lists exclusions, revision limits, milestones, payment schedule and the handover date. It also defines who owns each account and what support is available after launch. If a provider cannot produce a scope like this, ask for one before paying.

A sample handover agenda

Review the store together, confirm every integration works and walk through a test order from product page to confirmation email. Show where orders, customers, payments and supplier details live. Explain how to pause ads, update products and handle a refund. Record the session and share the notes.

Finish with a short list of open items and who owns each one. A handover is complete when you can operate the store without asking the provider for basic access or instructions. If anything is unclear, ask for a second walkthrough before the support window closes.

Key takeaways

  • Done-for-you buys time and structure, not guaranteed sales.
  • Get the scope, ownership and handover in writing.
  • Compare total cost, not the first invoice.
  • Keep control of your domain, accounts and data.
  • Choose a provider who answers hard questions clearly.

Final checklist before you hire

Confirm the scope, price, timeline, revision limit, ownership, support window and handover process. Ask for live proof and a clear answer about what happens if something breaks. If the provider cannot explain the process in plain language, keep looking. The right partner should make the project feel clearer, not more confusing, and should welcome a written comparison against another provider. A clear process is a better signal than a polished sales page. Ask for the process in writing and compare it with what actually happens before you make a final decision, and keep the comparison on file for the next time you review providers or need to explain the decision to a partner, investor or future team member without relying on memory or hunting through old messages and screenshots from months ago. Clear records make the next decision faster.
