Dropshipping Suppliers: A Beginner's Guide

By Akinyemi Ifeoluwa · September 15, 2026 · 8 min read

Your supplier is part of your brand whether you like it or not. When a package arrives late or damaged, the customer blames your store. Choose suppliers the way you would choose a business partner.

Types of suppliers

Local suppliers

Local suppliers can offer faster delivery, easier returns and better communication. The trade-off is usually higher cost and a smaller product range. For Nigerian stores selling to Nigerian customers, local suppliers can be a strong advantage.

International suppliers

International suppliers offer more products and lower unit costs. Delivery is longer and returns are harder. You need to set clear expectations and choose products that can survive the shipping journey.

Print-on-demand suppliers

Print-on-demand works for custom designs. It removes inventory risk but limits product types and can have longer production times. It is a good fit for apparel and gifts, not for every niche.

How to find suppliers

Start with supplier directories, marketplace searches and recommendations from other store owners. Then verify the supplier independently. A polished profile is not proof. Ask for recent references, shipping records and a sample.

Questions to ask before you sell

  • What is the real delivery window to my target country?
  • Who handles lost, damaged or wrong orders?
  • Can you provide tracking that updates reliably?
  • What is your return address and policy?
  • Can you hold stock if a product starts selling?
  • What happens during holidays or peak season?

Get the answers in writing. A supplier who avoids clear answers is a risk.

Test with a sample

Order the product yourself. Check quality, packaging, instructions and delivery time. If the sample is poor, do not convince yourself the customer experience will be better. It will not.

For skin-contact, children's or safety-related products, be stricter. Ask for material details and certifications where relevant.

Set up a backup supplier

Stock changes. A supplier can sell out, raise prices or stop shipping to your country. Keep at least one backup for your best product. Note the backup in your operations sheet so you are not searching in a panic.

Shipping expectations

Do not promise a delivery window you cannot control. Show a realistic range, explain that tracking may take time to update and give customers a support channel. Clear expectations reduce refund requests even when delivery is slow.

Returns and refunds

Decide your policy before launch. Who pays return shipping? What condition is required? How long does a refund take? Write it plainly and follow it consistently. A vague policy creates arguments.

Supplier red flags

  • Prices far below the market with no explanation.
  • No real contact information or business history.
  • Copy-pasted product descriptions and stolen images.
  • Pressure to pay outside the platform.
  • No tracking or no answer when an order goes wrong.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use multiple suppliers in one store?

Yes. It is common to use different suppliers for different products. Keep shipping profiles clear so customers are not surprised by split deliveries.

Should I dropship from AliExpress?

It can work for testing, but delivery times and quality vary. Vet the specific supplier and product, not the platform as a whole.

Do I need a contract?

A written agreement is useful for pricing, delivery, returns and responsibilities. At minimum, keep the terms in writing through email or chat.

How to vet a supplier step by step

Start with a written introduction: who you are, what you sell, expected volume and the markets you serve. Ask for product specifications, available stock, processing time, shipping options, tracking, packaging and return handling. Request a sample if the product value justifies it.

Then test the supplier on a small order. Check communication speed, accuracy, packaging and tracking. A supplier who is slow or vague before you pay will usually be worse after you have customers waiting.

Shipping and tracking promises

Ask for realistic delivery ranges, not best-case estimates. Confirm where the product ships from, which carriers are used, how tracking is provided and what happens when a parcel is delayed or lost. Put the promise in your store policies and leave a buffer for customs or local delivery delays.

Quality control and packaging

Inspect the product, not just the listing photo. Check size, materials, instructions, accessories and packaging. If the product is a gift, fragile or regulated, ask for extra protection and clear labelling. Poor packaging creates refunds that are far more expensive than a slightly higher supplier price.

Keeping continuity when a supplier fails

Identify a backup supplier before you need one. Keep product specifications and photos so a replacement can match the listing. If a supplier runs out of stock, pause ads instead of accepting orders you cannot fulfil. A short pause is cheaper than a wave of refunds and chargebacks.

Negotiation basics

Negotiate on total value, not just unit price. Ask about bulk shipping, packaging improvements, faster processing or better return terms. Be clear about your expected volume and pay on time. A reliable supplier is worth more than the cheapest quote.

A simple supplier scorecard

Score each supplier on product quality, communication, processing speed, shipping reliability, tracking, packaging, return handling and price. Review the scores after every twenty orders. The goal is a short list of dependable partners, not a long list of maybes.

How to onboard a new supplier

Send a clear onboarding document with product specifications, packaging requirements, label details and your support process. Ask the supplier to confirm each point in writing. Place a small test order and inspect it before you list the product as available.

Add the supplier's cut-off time and processing schedule to your internal notes so you do not promise delivery you cannot meet.

Inventory and stock monitoring

Check stock levels regularly, especially before a campaign. Ask how the supplier notifies you of outages and how quickly they can restock. If stock is unstable, reduce ad spend or pause the product rather than accepting orders you may not fulfil.

Communication templates

Keep short templates for order placement, tracking requests, damaged items and returns. Clear, calm messages get better results than repeated follow-ups. Record every commitment the supplier makes so you can refer back to it.

When to switch suppliers

Switch when quality drops repeatedly, when delays become normal, when tracking is unreliable or when communication breaks down. A cheaper supplier who causes refunds is more expensive than a reliable one. Migrate gradually and keep the old supplier available until the new one proves itself.

Shipping insurance and lost parcels

Ask who bears the risk when a parcel is lost or damaged. Some suppliers replace orders; others require you to file a claim. Know the process before a customer complains. If the supplier does not cover losses, include that risk in your pricing and refund policy.

Supplier performance review

Every month, review defect rate, processing time, delivery time, tracking accuracy and response time. Share the numbers with the supplier if the relationship is strong. A factual review is more useful than a vague complaint.

A sample order checklist

  • Order the exact variant a customer would receive.
  • Check packaging, labels and instructions.
  • Time the processing and delivery.
  • Test the tracking link.
  • Ask how a return would be handled.

How to handle returns with suppliers

Agree on who pays for return shipping, where returns are sent and how long a refund takes. For low-value items, a replacement or partial refund may be cheaper than a return. Put the policy in writing and make sure your store policy matches what the supplier will actually do.

Packaging and brand experience

Ask whether the supplier can include a thank-you card, a care note or custom packaging. Small touches improve the unboxing experience and reduce the feeling that the product came from an anonymous warehouse. Do not promise branded packaging unless the supplier has confirmed it.

A supplier onboarding checklist

  • Signed terms and contact details.
  • Product specifications and variant list.
  • Processing and shipping times.
  • Tracking and lost-parcel process.
  • Return and refund process.
  • Backup contact for urgent issues.

How to build a long-term supplier relationship

Pay on time, communicate clearly and avoid surprise chargebacks. Share useful feedback about product quality and customer questions. Suppliers prioritise reliable buyers when stock is tight, so professionalism is a practical advantage, not just good manners. Review the relationship every quarter and address small problems before they become expensive ones.

Final note

Treat suppliers as long-term operating partners. The cheapest quote is rarely the best deal once quality, delays and refunds are included. Reliability is worth paying for, and it becomes more valuable as your order volume grows.
